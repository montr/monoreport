## 9. Risks and approval decisions

1. Highest risk is XLSX scope identity and shared repetition multiplicity. The Stage 3 checkpoint makes correctness demonstrable before transport/UI integration. Reject irreconcilable demands under the approved contract rather than build a cardinality solver. A cheaper implementation that omits an approved supported case requires Product Owner approval; the auxiliary nature of samples does not silently reduce scope.
2. Compact descriptions can grow into a second engine. Keep them operation-local and limited to evidence, structure and counting. A small existing-helper exposure or source-inspection extraction is routine; broad binder/parser refactoring, a general IR or new shared validation infrastructure needs approval before proceeding.
3. Hierarchical defaults can exceed 1,000,000; this is an actionable failure, not permission to shrink counts. Arbitrary-size counts and pre-allocation saturation must coexist, including empty unknown arrays and horizontal empty products. The element limit does not bound all template/string memory.
4. Advisory hints do not encode formatter ranges or argument-specific constraints. Preserve generic fallback and warnings, not inference rules based on names or stricter Generate validation. Empty samples may require editing for numeric/date formatters; no universal render promise is added.
5. Inspection reuse and additive records carry backward-compatibility risk. Preserve signatures, deconstruction, per-occurrence information, source/diagnostic order, package ownership and all current template restrictions. Report a material contract/implementation/test discrepancy before changing behavior; none requiring a contract change was established during this planning inspection.
6. Review races require guards at response and acceptance, including dataset identity and revisions. Keep coordination in a focused hook; do not expand into a generic asynchronous-state framework.
7. The Product Owner approved this Implementation Plan and authorized implementation on 2026-11-08. No further product decision is currently unresolved. Existing architecture already settles HTTP names/status mapping, first-source conflicts, unknown-shape fallback and unavailable ancestors; do not reopen them without concrete contrary evidence.
8. Approval required only if encountered: materially changed scope/architecture/fallback, disproportionate approved edge-case handling, compatibility discrepancy, disruptive verification, or user-facing documentation edits. Present concrete alternatives and their verification impact before affected work proceeds.
